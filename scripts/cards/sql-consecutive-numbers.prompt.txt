Write a PostgreSQL query to find all numbers that appear at least three times consecutively in the `Logs` table.

### Schema: `Logs`
- `id` (INT, PRIMARY KEY): Auto-incrementing row identifier.
- `num` (INT): Logged numeric value.

### Query Contract
- Returns a column named `"ConsecutiveNums"` containing the distinct numbers that appear at least three consecutive times (where consecutive rows have sequential IDs `id`, `id + 1`, and `id + 2` with identical `num` values).
- Results must be ordered by `"ConsecutiveNums"` ascending.
- If no numbers repeat consecutively at least three times, return an empty result set (0 rows).

### Example
Given `Logs` records:
- `{ id: 1, num: 1 }`
- `{ id: 2, num: 1 }`
- `{ id: 3, num: 1 }`
- `{ id: 4, num: 2 }`
- `{ id: 5, num: 1 }`
- `{ id: 6, num: 2 }`
- `{ id: 7, num: 2 }`

The query returns:
- `[{ "ConsecutiveNums": 1 }]`

Notice that `1` is the only number that appears for at least three consecutive IDs (`1`, `2`, `3`). While `2` appears three times total, it only appears two times consecutively in rows 6 and 7.
